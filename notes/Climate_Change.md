- *Keynote — Kirsty Pringle: "We've Built a Great RSE Community. Now Let's Try to Save the Planet."* (RSECon26 keynote, Day 2, 10 Sept 2026, 09:00-09:45; `raw/File (1).md`; Dr Kirsty Pringle, Institute for Research Software/EPCC, University of Edinburgh — ex-climate scientist at the Met Office, Max Planck Institute, and University of Leeds, founder/chair of the Green Research Software Special Interest Group, NetDRIVE champion): Argues digital research's environmental footprint is largely invisible to the people generating it, even as institutions and funders set environmental policies whose day-to-day implications for RSEs stay unclear. - [Green DiSC: a Digital Sustainability Certification](https://www.software.ac.uk/GreenDiSC) (Institute for Research Software, `raw/Green DiSC_ a Digital Sustainability Certification _ Institute for Research Software.md`): The first open-access certification scheme giving research groups, computing teams, and institutions a roadmap for tackling the environmental impact of their computing — motivated by data centres' ~126 MT CO2e/year global footprint (comparable to all US commercial aviation) against an IPCC per-person 1.5°C budget of ~2 tonnes/year. Three applicant tracks (research groups, Research Computing Infrastructure teams, central sustainability teams) and three certification levels (Bronze live now, Silver rolling out, Gold due in autumn); criteria are open-access on GitHub, free to join, peer-audited (each applicant audits another), and built to be evidence-based, iterative, and community-sourced. Led by Loïc Lannelongue (Cambridge, also behind the Green Algorithms project) with Anica Araneta, and support from Kirsty Pringle, Denis Barclay, Shoaib Sufi, and Oscar Seip at the Institute for Research Software. Keywords: Green DiSC, digital sustainability certification, research computing, carbon footprint, Institute for Research Software. Related: [[Reproducible_Science]].

- [SparkHub Training – DRI Emissions](https://sdratraining.sparkhub.eu/) (`raw/SparkHub Training - DRI Emissions.md`): A ~30-minute self-paced course introducing the environmental impacts of digital research infrastructure (DRI) — devices, software, storage, networks, large-scale compute — covering greenhouse-gas emissions, material use, e-waste, and water use, and what researchers can practically do to reduce them. No prior DRI/emissions knowledge assumed; aimed at anyone using digital tools from a laptop up to institutional HPC. A concrete, hands-on companion to the Green DiSC certification and Kirsty Pringle's keynote above. Keywords: DRI emissions, digital research infrastructure, sustainability training, greenhouse gas emissions. Related: [[Reproducible_Science]].

- *Why Invest in Restoring Degraded Land?* (World Economic Forum Strategic Intelligence weekly digest, 1 Sept 2026, `raw/Gmail - Why Invest in Restoring Degraded Land_.md`): Spotlight framing land degradation as an economic challenge — up to 40% of the world's land affected, 3.2 billion people exposed, driven by unsustainable land use, short-term economic incentives, and climate change — timed to **UNCCD COP17** (the UN Convention to Combat Desertification's own COP17, Mongolia, 2026), a separate meeting from [[Conservation|CBD COP17 in Armenia]] already noted elsewhere in this vault — two distinct COP17s running in parallel this year. Links a WEF report, *The Hidden Economic Power of Healthy Soils*, framing soil health as an economic asset for productivity and resilience, plus an IUCN piece on bridging biodiversity, land degradation, and Loss & Damage, and a Project Syndicate piece, "The Global Land Agenda's Moment of Truth." Also features an interview with Tasso Azevedo (MapBiomas founder) on MapBiomas' ultra-detailed satellite land-cover maps of Brazil — see [[Remote_Sensing]] — which found the Pantanal wetland lost 60% of its water surface on average (vs. 15% for Brazil as a whole). Skipped two unrelated upcoming-webinar promos (cooling technologies, "K-shaped economy"). Keywords: land degradation, desertification, UNCCD COP17, soil economics, MapBiomas, Pantanal. Related: [[Conservation]], [[Remote_Sensing]].

- [FRB/Biodiversa+ press communiqué](https://www.biodiversa.eu): "Après un été marqué par la sécheresse, les incendies et les canicules, restaurer la nature est plus que jamais nécessaire" (26 August 2026, Fondation pour la recherche sur la biodiversité / Biodiversa+ / Water JPI). Timed to the EU member states' 1 September 2026 deadline to submit draft national ecosystem-restoration plans to the European Commission under the EU Nature Restoration Regulation (final plans adopted 2027, first revision only in 2032; France's own plan is titled "Agir pour restaurer la nature"), the release publishes eight policy briefs distilling a decade of European restoration research into practical guidance for national decision-makers and land managers. Four briefs cover ecosystem-specific roadmaps — integrating the biodiversity-climate-water nexus into forest-landscape restoration, marine and coastal ecosystem restoration, resilient urban waters (nature-based solutions for stormwater/flood pressure in urban catchments), and freshwater ecosystem restoration — and four cover enabling conditions: reducing zoonotic-disease risk through a "One Health" restoration lens, securing seed supply chains, biosecurity accreditation for tree/plant nurseries, and public-private financing partnerships. Two headline findings the briefs flag: fewer than one in ten national restoration policies currently addresses restoration's unintended health or social consequences, despite the well-established link between land-use change and infectious-disease emergence — see [[Safe_and_Just]] for this project's own social-foundations framing; and private financing of nature restoration in Europe remains marginal outside protected areas, against an estimated €65 billion/year investment need. Biodiversa+ co-chair Rainer Sodtke is quoted urging member states to use the briefs as evidence-based guidance while the regulation enters its "critical phase." Biodiversa+'s network spans 81 partner organisations across 39 countries (FRB among them) with €800M in funding over 2021-2028. Keywords: EU Nature Restoration Regulation, Biodiversa+, ecosystem restoration policy, One Health, restoration financing, climate resilience. Related: [[Conservation]], [[Political_Ecology]], [[Safe_and_Just]].
- *Renewable Energy Siting and the Côa Valley: Rewilding Portugal's SOPHIA Position* (`field-trips/deskStudy/renewable-energy-policy-coa-valley.md`): Rewilding Portugal's November 2025 opposition to the SOPHIA solar project (Fundão-Penamacor, 3,500 ha footprint, cutting the "Raia Norte"/"Raia Sul" ecological corridors) is not a blanket anti-solar position — it explicitly calls for siting on already-artificialised land (rooftops, brownfield) instead, backed by an environmental-justice argument (rural Beira Interior hosting transition costs without proportional benefit). Asks whether that project-specific statement should generalize into a standing policy position, and surveys three precedents: the Netherlands' 2020 "zonneladder" (solar ladder, rooftops-first — well-articulated but under-enforced), Germany's mature EEG spatial-control regime since 2004, and Portugal's own live RED III transposition (the "Mapa Verde" go-to-areas consultation, June 2026) as the concrete process this question could plug into now. See [[Urban_Planning]] for the companion solar-siting/tourism-lodge infrastructure proposal this note leads into. Keywords: RED III, solar siting, ecological corridors, environmental justice, Mapa Verde, SOPHIA. Related: [[Safe_and_Just]], [[Urban_Planning]].

- *Fire recurrence as a landscape-stability precondition*: draft paper abstract #12 (`portfolio/paper-abstracts-draft.md`, updated 25 Aug 2026 — see [[Conservation]]) contrasts MODIS MCD64A1.061 burn records for the same 2015–mid-2025 window across two study areas: zero detected burn pixels within Fontainebleau's 153 km² core forest, France, against a single July 2026 wildfire burning 5,000+ ha across three Côa Valley municipalities in two days. Argues fire recurrence should be a stated applicability precondition, not an optional covariate, for any resistance-surface method proposed for transfer across European rewilding landscapes — Fontainebleau's own tiered strict-reserve governance model implicitly assumes a decade-scale stability the Côa Valley cannot. Keywords: fire ecology, MODIS, landscape stability, comparative case study, resistance surface.
