# Critical Raw Materials

**Summary**: The EU's Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA) and how it is being put into practice: benchmarks for extraction, processing and recycling, the Strategic Projects selected under it, and the duties it creates for permitting, site restoration, biodiversity and open geodata.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-25 (page created from the CRMA document set: Regulation 2024/1252, the 2023 proposal and impact assessment, the RESourceEU action plan, and the second wave of 46 Strategic Projects, 9 Oct 2026)

- [Commission Decision C(2026)8051 recognising 46 new Strategic Projects](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/56f5457c-2ed0-4c10-8c76-d66a79cb00b6_en?filename=C_2026_8051_F1_COMMISSION_DECISION_EN_V2_P1_5091203.PDF) and its [annex](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/document/download/efabd887-9c12-4a3d-910e-8433c0f63a81_en?filename=C_2026_8051_F1_ANNEX_EN_V3_P1_5091583.PDF) (9 Oct 2026; with a 3-page overview table from the Publications Office). This is the second wave, all inside the EU: 46 projects in 16 Member States, needing €21.1bn of investment. By type: 8 extraction, 11 processing, 19 recycling, 8 combined. They cover 15 of the 17 strategic raw materials; bismuth and boron are missing, by my count from the annex.
  - France has six projects: Lithium de France, EMME, NEOMAT-PCAM, LUERN (Aubert & Duval), BREES (Rhodia), and a share of CRM4Defence (Leonardo).
  - Portugal has the Borralha tungsten mine. Spain has a tungsten plant at Abenójar and the Cobre San Rafael copper mine.
  - Recital 7 says outside experts assessed the applications on technical, financial, environmental, social and governance grounds. The annex lists every project as "ongoing", with no finer stage.
  - The application numbers don't match: the press release says 102 were received, the Decision says 101.
  - Keywords: strategic projects, CRMA, recycling, lithium, tungsten, France.

- [EU Energy and Raw Materials Platform: Raw Materials Mechanism](https://energy-platform.ec.europa.eu/raw-materials) (European Commission). The joint-purchasing and offtake-matching service set up under Articles 17 and 25 of the CRMA. It covers all 17 strategic raw materials and runs in rounds: diversification, project development, and storage and stockpiling. Registration opened on 18 Nov 2025. By October 2026 the Commission reported more than 30 matches for 10 materials.
  - Keywords: joint purchasing, offtake, demand aggregation, platform.

- [RESourceEU Action Plan, COM(2025)945](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52025DC0945) ("Accelerating our critical raw materials strategy to adapt to a new reality", 3 Dec 2025, 19 pp). The Commission's plan to speed up the CRMA.
  - **New body:** a European Critical Raw Materials Centre, modelled on Japan's JOGMEC, starting in 2026.
  - **Money:** InvestEU about €2bn for 2026–27, the Innovation Fund at least €700m, up to €300m of the Battery Booster for raw materials projects, and Horizon Europe €593m.
  - **Rules:** guidance on the Water Framework Directive, export limits on magnet scrap, a stockpiling pilot, and FDI screening for strategic projects.
  - **Skills:** a skills partnership with a Raw Materials Academy run by EIT RawMaterials.
  - Keywords: RESourceEU, financing, stockpiling, skills, action plan.

- [Regulation (EU) 2024/1252, the Critical Raw Materials Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1252/oj) (11 Apr 2024, 67 pp).
  - **2030 benchmarks** (Art. 5): EU capacity for 10% of yearly needs in extraction, 40% in processing and 25% in recycling, and no more than 65% from any single third country.
  - **Lists:** 17 strategic and 34 critical raw materials (Annexes I–II), to be reviewed by 24 May 2027.
  - **Permitting:** strategic projects get time limits of 27 months for extraction and 15 months for processing or recycling (Art. 11), and one national contact point each.
  - **Restoration:** extraction projects outside the EU must have a site-restoration plan (Art. 7(1)(h)). Recognised certification schemes must cover biodiversity and the project's life "before, during and after closure" (Art. 30, Annex IV).
  - **Open geodata:** Art. 27 requires a public database of closed and abandoned extractive-waste sites. Art. 19 requires national exploration programmes with free public mineral maps. Recital 43 points to Earth observation for monitoring mining areas.
  - **Governance and monitoring:** a European Critical Raw Materials Board with a subgroup of national geological surveys, and stress tests every three years.
  - Relevant to [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Data]] and [[Conservation]].
  - Keywords: CRMA, regulation, benchmarks, permitting, extractive waste, certification.

- [Proposal for a Critical Raw Materials Act, COM(2023)160](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52023PC0160) (16 Mar 2023, with annexes, financial statement and the Commission's 4-page factsheet). The Commission's first text, written by DG GROW. It proposed a 15% recycling benchmark, which the final Regulation raised to 25%. It estimated up to 33 full-time Commission staff a year to run the Act.
  - Keywords: CRMA, proposal, legislative history, DG GROW.

- [Impact assessment of the Critical Raw Materials Act, SWD(2023)161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52023SC0161) (16 Mar 2023, 203 pp), with its executive summary SWD(2023)162, the subsidiarity grid SWD(2023)160, and the Regulatory Scrutiny Board's opinion SEC(2023)360 (18 Jan 2023: "positive with reservations"). The evidence base behind the Act. It draws heavily on the Joint Research Centre and its Raw Materials Information System.
  - It names mining rehabilitation as a skills gap, and sees re-mining of old waste sites as a chance to restore them.
  - It suggests hyperspectral and multispectral data for exploration, and cites certification schemes such as IRMA.
  - The social and environmental side of these choices is a theme of [[Political_Ecology]] and [[Environmental_Justice]].
  - Keywords: impact assessment, JRC, rehabilitation, skills, certification.
