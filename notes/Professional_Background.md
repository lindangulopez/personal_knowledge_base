- [How AI will Reshape the Geospatial Job Market](https://geoawesome.com/geospatial-workforce-ai-skills-spatial-judgment/) (Geoawesome, 15 Aug 2026, by Aleks Buczkowski, PwC Drone Powered Solutions/Geospatial Hub): Argues AI isn't eliminating geospatial work but shifting what counts as expertise — routine production (digitizing, boilerplate scripting, first-pass classification) is automatable, while judgment (validating whether a workflow is geographically defensible) is becoming more valuable, not less. Evidence: 2025 O*NET job-posting analysis shows ArcGIS still dominant (75% of GIS-technologist postings) but Python (34%), SQL (22%) and JavaScript (13%) now routinely co-required, and BLS projects cartographer/photogrammetrist employment growing 6% (2024-2034) while the narrower "geographer" title declines 3% — read as a classification problem, not a shrinking field, since spatial work is spreading into titles like data scientist and climate-risk analyst. The author's own hiring criteria (PwC): real project experience demonstrating spatial reasoning, data-quality/uncertainty judgment, and the ability to challenge an AI-generated result, not just tool proficiency — "a technically weak practitioner equipped with AI may simply produce incorrect answers faster." Proposes a five-layer future-ready curriculum: spatial foundations, computational practice (Python/SQL/APIs/Git/cloud/reproducible pipelines), AI literacy (including hallucination, bias, provenance, cost), domain fluency, and professional judgment (ethics, communication, stakeholder engagement). Also names an "entry-level paradox": automating away tedious junior tasks (digitizing, address-cleaning) removes the exact exposure to messy real data that builds senior judgment, unless junior roles are redesigned around supervised automation and evaluation rather than eliminated outright. See [[Remote_Sensing]] and [[Agentic_Coding]] for the technical-tooling side of this; a private CV-positioning note drawing on this article and the RSE piece above lives in the private vault. Keywords: geospatial workforce, GeoAI, hybrid skills, spatial judgment, curriculum, hiring criteria. Related: [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Agentic_Coding]].

- *RSEcon26 CodeMeta Workshop — Zenodo deposit*: Linda co-authored and presented "Making Research Software FAIR with CodeMeta [Part I]", published to Zenodo 31 Aug 2026 (DOI [10.5281/zenodo.22210982](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22210982); all-versions DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22210981), a Presentation-type deposit dual-licensed CC BY 4.0 / CC BY-SA 4.0, for the Research Software Engineering Conference 2026 (RSECon26), Sheffield, UK, 9-11 September 2026 (session "Making Research Software FAIR with CodeMeta, Part I & II" — a Part II companion record also exists as an additional title on this record). Co-authors/roles: Esteban Gonzalez (contact person), Linda Angulo Lopez (contact person), Anas El Hounsri (annotator), Aida Jafarbigloo (editor). The interactive workshop teaches CodeMeta's schema and core concepts as a standardized, machine-readable (JSON-LD/schema.org) metadata layer that counters research-software metadata fragmentation across repositories and tools, covering: automatically generating `codemeta.json` files (GitHub Actions, the autocodemeta tool, SOMEF); avoiding common metadata pitfalls; mapping metadata from other schemas (package managers, repository metadata) into CodeMeta via structured crosswalks and the emerging SSSOM (Simple Standard for Sharing Ontological Mappings) practice; and using LLMs to help generate those mappings. Targets researchers, RSEs, and developers improving software-metadata quality/integration across infrastructures like Zenodo and Software Heritage. Session materials attached to the record: slides (`RSEcon26_FAIR_SWH_CodeMeta.pptx`/PDF), a mapping-methodology handout (`RSEcon26_Mapping_Intro_Method`), and a welcome deck; also references the [CodeMeta Generator](https://codemeta.github.io/codemeta-generator/) tool. This is a separate engagement from the CodeMeta talk at ECCB 2026 (below) — same underlying CodeMeta/Software Heritage expertise, different conference and co-authors. See [[Reproducible_Science]] for related CodeMeta/SWHID background (Di Cosmo's "Beyond FAIR Data" course) and IRSC26/RSECon26 co-location logistics. Source: `raw/screencapture-zenodo-org-records-22210982-2026-08-31-18_34_05-edit.pdf`. Keywords: CodeMeta, RSEcon26, Zenodo, FAIR software metadata, SSSOM, Software Heritage, crosswalks.
