- [UN resolution urges reparations for slavery's 'historical wrongs'](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/03/1167199) (UN News, 25 Mar 2026): On the International Day of Remembrance of Victims of Slavery and the Transatlantic Slave Trade, the General Assembly adopted a Ghana-led resolution declaring the trafficking of enslaved Africans and their racialised chattel enslavement the gravest crime against humanity (123 for, 3 against, 52 abstentions), and affirming reparations claims as a concrete step towards remedy. Annalena Baerbock called it "mass resource extraction"; António Guterres asked states to respect African countries' ownership of their natural resources and their equal say in the global financial architecture and the Security Council; the US said it recognises no legal right to reparations for acts that were legal at the time. Keywords: reparations, gravest crime against humanity, Ghana, African Group, natural resources, General Assembly. Related: [[Critical_Raw_Materials]], [[Political_Ecology]].

- [A/80/325: Programme of activities for the implementation of the International Decade for People of African Descent](https://undocs.org/A/80/325) (Report of the Secretary-General, 8 Aug 2025, 19 pages): The first report under the Second International Decade (2025-2034), built on 28 submissions from states and other stakeholders. It reviews action under the three pillars of recognition, justice and development, multiple and aggravated discrimination, and regional and international developments (the African Union's 2025 theme "Justice for Africans and people of African descent through reparations", CERD's work on a general recommendation on reparations, COP16's first recognition of the role of people of African descent in conserving biodiversity, the FAO/ECLAC report on rural communities). It then turns to current trends: displacement by extractive megaprojects and mining in Colombia, evictions and land dispossession, threats to land and environmental defenders, and the disproportionate burden of the climate and ecological crises. The conclusions call for reparatory justice at the centre of the Decade, collective rights to "lands, territories and resources", data disaggregated by race under a human-rights-based approach to [[Data]] (para. 58), reform of the international financial architecture including debt justice, removing bias from AI, and climate finance and technology transfer as reparatory measures (para. 63). Keywords: Second International Decade, reparatory justice, land rights, disaggregated data, environmental injustice, A/80/325. Related: [[Environmental_Justice]], [[Political_Ecology]], [[Climate_Change]].

- [OHCHR and the Second International Decade for People of African Descent 2025-2034](https://www.ohchr.org/en/racism/second-international-decade-african-descent) (OHCHR): The hub page for the Decade proclaimed by resolution 79/193 (17 Dec 2024) under the theme "recognition, justice and development", with the three objectives carried over from resolution 69/16. OHCHR coordinates it: support to states, the annual Fellowship programme for people of African descent, the Secretary-General's yearly reports, the Anti-Discrimination database, and calls for inputs. Keywords: OHCHR, Second International Decade, 79/193, fellowship, calls for input. Related: [[Environmental_Justice]].

- [The Second International Decade for People of African Descent renews the call for recognition, justice, and development](https://www.ohchr.org/en/stories/2024/12/second-international-decade-people-african-descent-renews-call-recognition-justice) (OHCHR story, 19 Dec 2024): Experts judge that the first Decade (2015-2024) raised awareness and created the Permanent Forum on People of African Descent, but lacked political will and investment. Barbara Reynolds (Working Group of Experts) calls for reparatory, environmental and digital justice; June Soomer (Permanent Forum) links systemic racism to climate vulnerability and environmental injustice. OHCHR's plan for the second Decade includes disaggregated data, participation in decision-making, and action on environmental and digital injustice. Keywords: first Decade review, Permanent Forum, WGEPAD, environmental justice, digital justice. Related: [[Environmental_Justice]], [[Safe_and_Just]].

- *Land to Sea: Ocean Currents Connecting Portugal to Brazil, Angola, and Mozambique* (`field-trips/deskStudy/portugal-land-to-sea-ocean-currents-colonial-routes.md`): Reads Portugal's Age-of-Discovery navigation as a historical instance of the [[Conservation|eco-connectivity notebook]]'s own circuit-theory method, applied at ocean-basin scale centuries before GIS existed to model it. The *volta do mar* technique used the North Atlantic Gyre's clockwise circulation to make the West Africa/return-leg voyage possible at all; Cabral's 1500 landfall in Brazil was driven by the South Atlantic Gyre's counter-clockwise currents, not luck. The Benguela Current, which blocked 15th-century ships headed past Angola, is today one of the world's four major upwelling systems (~20% of global fish catches) and a documented warming hotspot threatening Angolan coastal food security. The Agulhas Current sustains Mozambique's coral reefs (Inhambane, Quirimbas) via the Mozambique Channel. The Portuguese Current still actively transports microplastics along Portugal's own coast today — the same ocean-current connectivity, now carrying something else. Keywords: volta do mar, ocean gyres, Benguela Current, Agulhas Current, colonial navigation, ocean connectivity. Related: [[Conservation]], [[Climate_Change]].
- *Colonial Water and Land Policy: Ecological Legacies in Brazil, Mozambique, and Angola* (`field-trips/deskStudy/portugal-colonial-ecological-policy-brazil-mozambique-angola.md`): Reads Portugal's colonial-era environmental footprint through Indigenous Science and community-led resistance as the standpoint that names what colonial/extractive policy actually did, not a neutral state-comparison. Brazil: Atlantic Forest cleared from first contact while the Amazon interior stayed peripheral until later, followed by a real post-independence policy reversal (Forest Codes, PPCDAm satellite monitoring, deforestation down from 27,772 km²/2004 to 5,796 km²/2025) undercut by a rising share of fire-driven "progressive degradation." Mozambique: the present-day **ProSavana** agribusiness project on the Nacala Corridor risked displacing ~4 million small-scale farmers, substantially reproducing colonial export-crop-over-subsistence logic, until more than five years of cross-border resistance (Mozambique's UNAC and Justiça Ambiental alongside Brazil's own Landless Workers' Movement, MST) curtailed it — community organizing, not state policy, constrained it. Angola: a genuinely progressive late-colonial conservation start (a 1911 hunting-licence-funded conservation fund, ~6% of land protected by 1975) collapsed entirely in the civil war's vacuum, filled by diamond/oil extraction as the colonial economy's direct successor. Keywords: colonial ecology, ProSavana, land grabs, Amazon deforestation, Angola conservation collapse, community resistance. Related: [[Safe_and_Just]], [[Political_Ecology]].
- *Portugal's Rivers and Seas: A National Water History* (`field-trips/deskStudy/portugal-water-history-rivers-seas.md`): Grounding note for the two entries above — everything else this project had documented about water was Côa/Douro-basin only. The Tejo (1,007 km, 440 m³/s mean discharge at its estuary) and the Douro (~505 m³/s) both dwarf the Côa (~2,495 km² drainage basin, an order of magnitude smaller). Portugal's national hydrological monitoring network, SNIRH, is documented as built for flood evaluation and explicitly not equipped for a "unique" drought classification — structural under-monitoring of a country that regularly experiences severe water scarcity. Frames Portugal's Atlantic-facing maritime identity (developed further in the ocean-currents entry above) as inseparable from its riverine one. Keywords: SNIRH, Tejo, Douro discharge, drought monitoring gap, national water history. Related: [[Climate_Change]].
