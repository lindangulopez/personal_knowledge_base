# Citing and preserving research code: four talks in 2026

Between September and October 2026 I gave four talks on the same question: when a paper depends on code, how do we keep that code, and cite it?

The answer I teach has two parts. A SWHID, the Software Heritage identifier, points to an exact version of the code, archived for the long term. A CodeMeta file describes the software: who wrote it, its licence, how to cite it.

The four talks, each on Zenodo:

- (i) *Making Research Software FAIR with CodeMeta*, RSECon26, Sheffield, with Esteban Gonzalez and the CodeMeta team. [doi:10.5281/zenodo.22298094](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22298094)
- (ii) *Preserving the Code Behind Bioinformatics*, ECCB 2026, ELIXIR workshop. [doi:10.5281/zenodo.22815141](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22815141)
- (iii) *Preserving the Code of Biodiversity*, AI4OMICS, Jardin des Plantes, Paris, 2 October.
- (iv) *Citer et préserver des codes et logiciels avec SWHID et CodeMeta*, CNRS SIST webinar, 15 October. [doi:10.5281/zenodo.22831096](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22831096)

Each audience was different: research software engineers, bioinformaticians, biodiversity scientists, and French research-support staff. The question was the same. Is the code behind your last paper archived?
