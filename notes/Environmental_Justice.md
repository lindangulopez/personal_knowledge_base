# Environmental Justice

**Summary**: Who bears environmental harm and who gets a say: the human right to a healthy environment, the rights of future generations, and fair access to environmental decisions and remedies.
**Last updated**: 2026-11-12 (Greenpeace Africa: how the deCOALonize coalition stopped the Lamu coal plant); 2026-11-04 (Greenpeace Africa on COP31: polluters pay, grants not debt); 2026-10-25 (call for inputs on land rights of people of African descent; pointer to the Second International Decade entries)

- *David vs. Goliath: How Greenpeace Africa and the deCOALonize movement defeated the Lamu Coal Plant* (Fredrick Njehu, Pan-African Political Strategist, Greenpeace Africa Updates newsletter on LinkedIn, email of 12 Nov 2026; the email gives the opening only, the LinkedIn link is a tracking redirect and was not followed): how a proposed $2 billion, 1,050 MW coal plant on the coast of Lamu, Kenya, next to a UNESCO World Heritage site and fragile marine ecosystems, was stopped. Written for Greenpeace's 20 years in Africa, it credits a coalition that split the work by strength: Greenpeace Africa on political engagement, direct action and scientific research (Njehu chaired the deCOALonize board); Katiba Institute and Natural Justice on the legal challenges, grounded in constitutional rights and environmental law; 350.org and Save Lamu on grassroots mobilisation; Sauti ya Wanawake and Save Lamu with women and youth networks in Lamu and Kitui, so that affected communities led the narrative; plus a strategy to cut the project's funding at source. Communities from Lamu and Kitui also delivered letters to Kenya's Ministry of Energy and the Chinese Embassy. Keywords: Lamu coal plant, deCOALonize, Kenya, climate justice, coalition strategy, coal finance. Related: [[Climate_Change]], [[Political_Ecology]].


- [From rhetoric to reality: Why COP31 must deliver for Africa](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-rhetoric-reality-why-cop31-must-deliver-africa-frpxf) (Fred Njehu, Pan African Political Strategist, Greenpeace Africa Updates newsletter on LinkedIn, 4 Nov 2026; article read in full): ahead of COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, Greenpeace Africa asks for an "implementation COP", not "another talk shop". The fossil fuel industry should pay for climate damage, the polluter-pays principle linked to the UN Tax Convention talks. It calls for at least 75% renewable power worldwide by 2035, with no gas as a "transition fuel", and treats 1.5°C as "a non-negotiable hard limit for African survival". Africa needs an estimated US$1.3-1.6 trillion in climate finance through 2030. It gets less than 15% of the adaptation funding it needs each year, and nearly 70% of what arrives comes as loans that add to debt. So adaptation and loss and damage should be paid in grants, funded by taxes on fossil-fuel windfall profits and wealthy polluters, under the New Collective Quantified Goal agreed in Baku. The article ends by backing Ethiopia's COP32 presidency in Addis Ababa. Keywords: COP31, climate finance, polluter pays, loss and damage, Africa, grants not debt. Related: [[Climate_Change]], [[Decolonial_Ecology]], [[Political_Ecology]].
