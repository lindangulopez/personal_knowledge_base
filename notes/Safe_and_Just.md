# Safe and Just

**Summary**: The "safe and just" framing (planetary boundaries + social foundations) applied to conservation and rewilding decisions — where ecological limits and equity of access/benefit are assessed together rather than separately.

- *"The Starter" — Global Artivism Month's Recipe Book 01, now fully captured* (`raw/private/coa-connectivitylab/1788359128the-starter-recipe-book-global-artivism-month-1-1-pdf1788359128.pdf`, the first of six Global Artivism Month recipe books, "How to Take Part in Global Artivism Month," for artists, artivists, collectives, organizations, and communities everywhere): Supersedes the thin 1-of-16-pages capture in the "Global Artivism Month — invitation to collaborate" entry below with the movement's own account of itself. Global Artivism is a Global South/Global Majority-led movement connecting artists, cultural practitioners, organizers, storytellers and activists creating change through culture; Global Artivism Month is its most visible moment, grown out of two prior in-person gatherings (Pretoria 2024, Salvador da Bahia 2025) and building toward the movement's next global gathering in 2027. Structure: **three ways of taking part** — Witness (follow and amplify, no production required), Host (organize an event or activity at any scale, appears in the shared calendar and gets host materials), Create (contribute one piece of work — poetry, music, mural, performance, photography, fashion, installation, film, food, sport-as-culture — credited, and eligible to be featured during your region's moment in the relay) — plus **two ways of making it possible**, sitting alongside rather than inside the three: Support (resource someone else's work — funding, equipment, space, skills, connections; Global Artivism itself does not fund activities or distribute grants) and Partner (commit an organisation, network or platform in a sustained, case-by-case-agreed role — co-organizer, mobilization partner, media partner, or workshop host for the wider network). No jury, no selection; participation is free, framed around positive social impact rather than a political test; only a short Terms and Conditions document applies (purpose, safety, consent, use of the Global Artivism name). **Five-step join process**: (1) register interest via the Participation Form, open 19 Aug-19 Oct 2026 — a registration, not an application; (2) shape your activity, at any scale — "one person with one poem is a valid activity, so is a month-long program"; (3) meet your region at one of six virtual regional convenings (Americas & Caribbean, Africa, Europe, West Asia, Oceania & Pacific, Asia) held late Aug-early Oct, each launching one recipe book with a short activation workshop; (4) prepare with the six recipe books (this being book 01; later titles cover fundraising, access and participation, youth artivism, regenerative practices, and delivery/documentation, per the "What's Inside" table of contents) — all six then edited into one Global Artivism Month Cookbook, released the first week of November; (5) show up — hold the activity, document it, feed it into the daily dispatch. This is the movement-level context behind Linda's own now-live [[Cartography|"Movement, Water, Connection" Côa Valley gallery]] and its [[Conservation|call for lab participants]] — see those pages for her own project's application of this recipe. Keywords: Global Artivism Month, The Starter, recipe book, Sunrise Relay, regional convenings, Participation Form, Global Artivism Cookbook. Related: [[Cartography]], [[Conservation]], [[Political_Ecology]].

- *Whose "Safe and Just"? Indigenous Science, Earth System Justice, and a Correction* (`field-trips/deskStudy/safe-and-just-indigenous-science-earth-system-justice.md`): Corrects a citation error the eco-connectivity notebook had carried since v3 — "safe and just" is not Kate Raworth's 2012 doughnut-economics model, and its origin isn't a Western academic paper at all. The underlying idea (living within ecological limits while upholding justice and right relationship with land) comes from Indigenous Science and community-led climate justice movements, per Jessica Hernandez's *Fresh Banana Leaves* (2022). The project's actual quantified framework draws on Joyeeta Gupta's 2023 Earth Commission work (Rockström et al., "Safe and just Earth system boundaries," *Nature* 2023; Gupta et al., "Earth system justice," *Nature Sustainability* 2023) as the academic articulation *downstream* of that older idea — not its source. Also names a live epistemic-justice critique of the planetary-boundaries lineage itself (a self-selected group of scientists defining humanity's "safe space" without input from humanity). Raworth is no longer cited anywhere in the project. Keywords: Indigenous Science, Jessica Hernandez, Earth system boundaries, Joyeeta Gupta, epistemic justice, citation correction. Related: [[Decolonial_Ecology]].
- *Ten Social Foundations and Ten Planetary Boundaries* (`field-trips/deskStudy/safe-and-just-social-foundations-planetary-boundaries.md`): Operationalizes the corrected "safe and just" framing at finer grain — ten social foundations (food security, health & education, income/work/AI, peace & justice, political voice, social & gender equity, climate migration, capital, energy poverty, water security) and ten planetary boundaries (climate change, ocean acidification, chemical pollution, nitrogen/phosphorus loading, freshwater withdrawals, land conversion, genetic biodiversity loss, biodiversity loss by disturbance, air pollution/ozone, new entities), both extending Rockström et al. 2009's original nine-boundary/twelve-foundation model. Ties two boundaries — climate change and biodiversity loss — directly to the project's own work: Côa Valley fire-barrier modeling, Portugal's structural drought-monitoring gap, Amazon deforestation's improving-but-fire-shifting trend, the Benguela Current's Angola-fisheries warming threat, and Mozambique's Quirimbas coral reefs. Keywords: planetary boundaries, social foundations, Rockström, biodiversity loss, climate change boundary. Related: [[Decolonial_Ecology]], [[Climate_Change]].
- *Eco-tourism siting and human-wildlife conflict, read against the corridor*: From the Côa Valley illustration project's [[Conservation|Post-Trip Development Plan]] (`field-notes/Post-Development-Plan.md`). Extends the eco-connectivity notebook's Survey123 farmer-conflict tracking (fences, livestock, agricultural boundaries) to the recreational side: visitors at the praia fluviais (Rapoula, Vale das Éguas) and the Fóios trail share corridor with less-visible fauna (wildcat, otter, wild boar) — a stewardship/signage question (seasonal notices, leashed dogs near otter holts), not an argument against access, since the connectivity model shows habitat/infrastructure overlap but says nothing about actual encounter risk. On siting: candidate eco-tourism development should avoid the combined connectivity map's "pinch points" (narrow high-flow corridor stretches where added infrastructure does disproportionate fragmentation damage), and — the *just* half — should be checked against reachability from more than one municipality (the project's own visited sites already span Almeida, Sabugal, and Vila Nova de Foz Côa) rather than concentrating investment wherever is currently easiest to reach. Explicitly flags what it does *not* claim: no demographic, disability-access, or visitor-origin data exists in the project, so "just" here means multi-site reachability and not concentrating access at the corridor's expense — not an assessed equity/inclusion claim. Proposes three concrete recommendations to Rewilding Portugal: (i) a pre-commitment siting screen against the connectivity map's pinch points, (ii) seasonal stewardship signage at Rapoula/Vale das Éguas (current overlap, not a future risk), and (iii) a "reach over convenience" principle for future investment. Keywords: safe and just, eco-tourism siting, human-wildlife conflict, planetary boundaries, social foundations, connectivity pinch points. Related: [[Conservation]].
