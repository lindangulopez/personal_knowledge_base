- *Evolving the RSE role in the age of generative and agentic AI* (RSECon26 talk, Day 1, 9 Sept 2026, 11:00-11:15, Lecture Theatre 1, The Wave, session #144; `raw/File.md`, [Zenodo record 22230055](https://zenodo.org/records/22230055), `raw/Evolving the RSE role in the age of generative and agentic AI.md`, `RSECon26_Evolving_RSE_role_GenAI_slides.pdf`; Stephan Druskat, Ben van Werkhoven, Daniel S. Katz, Ian A. Cosden, Joseph Shingleton, Michelle Barker, Neil Chue Hong, Robert Haines, Rohan Marwaha, Sandra Gesing — TU Braunschweig, Leiden, UIUC, Princeton, Glasgow, ReSA, Edinburgh/SSI, Manchester, UC San Diego/US-RSE; funded by Schmidt Sciences grant G-25-69965 and UKRI grant AH/Z000114/1): Already named in the Day-1-3 programme summary logged elsewhere on this page; now given its own entry. As GenAI lets researchers generate code with less programming experience, RSE collaborations are anecdotally shifting toward higher-level work (code review, prompt engineering, configuring agentic workflows) — raising the open question of whether the RSE role shrinks to reviewing AI-generated codebases or whether agentic systems eventually replace RSE groups outright. Developed at a ReSA workshop and a IRSC26 Birds-of-a-Feather session, the talk argues the RSE role's value survives because RSEs uniquely combine research-domain understanding with professional software-engineering expertise — the thing that keeps AI-accelerated research software both sustainable and reproducible — and that realising that value means RSEs increasingly building GenAI-method expertise into their own practice. Proposes a community roadmap for developing measures to future-proof the role. Directly complementary to the RAIRR risk-register entry elsewhere on this page (same "GenAI changes RSE practice" moment, opposite angle — role/value versus risk cataloguing). Keywords: RSE role, generative AI, agentic AI, ReSA workshop, future of research software engineering. Related: [[Reproducible_Science]], [[Professional_Background]].

- *Detecting novel biothreats from global news with AI – from research to production deployment in the Cabinet Office National Situation Centre* (RSECon26 talk, Day 2, 10 Sept 2026, 11:00-11:30, Lecture Theatre 2, The Wave, session #83; `raw/File (3).md`; Drew Silcock, Daniel Clayton — STFC Hartree Centre; Alex Mason — Cabinet Office, UK): The National Situation Centre (SitCen, part of Cabinet Office Briefing Rooms) must catch potential new biothreats in global news as early as possible, at a data volume that makes manual review impossible. Presents **Biothreat Media Analyser (BMA)**, an STFC Hartree Centre/SitCen collaboration using frontier embedding models to continuously scan world news and flag potentially novel biothreats, built jointly by RSEs, data scientists, and data engineers. Frames the whole project as a productionisation case study — the journey from ideation through research/exploration to iteration and production deployment, the ongoing two-way relationship between researchers and the RSEs turning research into production code, the architectural decisions needed to move from notebooks analysing static data to algorithms running continuously on live data (and their trade-offs), and lessons from delivering research software into a high-stakes, information-sensitive government department. Keywords: embedding models, AI productionisation, biothreat detection, Cabinet Office, STFC Hartree Centre, research-to-production pipeline. Related: [[Reproducible_Science]], [[Machine_Learning]].

- *Report of the 2025 Workshop on Next-Generation Ecosystems for Scientific Computing* ([arXiv:2510.03413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03413), Argonne National Laboratory report ANL-25/47, workshop held Chicago IL, 29 Apr–1 May 2025, report published 26 Sept 2025; lead author Lois Curfman McInnes with 30+ co-authors spanning Argonne, LLNL, LANL, ORNL, PNNL, Sandia, several universities, and industry (Ford Motor, GE Aerospace); `raw/private/2510.03413v2.pdf`, full text now read — corrects/expands the secondhand summary Linda's own GitHub Discussion used, see [[Conservation]]): the paper's actual title is *"Toward Next-Generation Ecosystems for Scientific Computing: Harnessing Community, Software, and AI for Cross-Disciplinary Team Science"* — "Reimagining Scientific Computing in the Age of AI" (the framing used in Linda's Côa pipeline discussion) is the heading of its own Executive Summary, not the report's title. 40+ experts across HPC, AI, computational science, applied math, and social/community-development science. Central methodology is **socio-technical co-design** — treating social and technical components of a scientific-computing ecosystem as interdependent, not separable — illustrated by a figure ringing Researchers/Software-Developers/Stakeholders/Computing-Facilities with Software, Collaboration, and Pedagogy, itself wrapped in AI, Team-based Science, Co-design, and Community & Workforce Development. Three challenge areas structure the whole report, each with a matching "required research directions and community actions" section: (2.1/3.1) software ecosystems for AI in scientific computing; (2.2/3.2) cross-disciplinary collaboration and AI for scientific software teams; (2.3/3.3) pedagogy and workforce development in the age of AI; plus (2.4/3.4) cross-cutting interdependencies and required community actions. Two key stated themes: AI is not just another tool — it can dramatically boost scientific productivity, but its own evolution is unknown; and supporting this transformation needs a broad, cross-disciplinary community and sustained investment in both today's and tomorrow's researchers. Linda used the report's own condensed recommendations (trustworthy modular software even with AI-written parts, disciplined-but-not-restrictive team AI use, continuous training, shared AI compute, cross-disciplinary teaching, written responsible-AI guidelines, public-private partnership pilots) as the explicit frame for a governance discussion on her own Côa Valley pipeline, asking which ideas transfer to a project built for resource-limited conservation groups rather than a national lab — see [[Conservation]] for the nine concrete proposals that came out of it. Keywords: AI in scientific computing, socio-technical co-design, research software ecosystems, workforce development, Argonne workshop report. Related: [[Conservation]], [[Reproducible_Science]].
